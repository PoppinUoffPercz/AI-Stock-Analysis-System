## 1. Background

Michael Burry is a physician-turned-investor who founded Scion Capital in 2000. He gained fame for being one of the first to recognize the subprime mortgage bubble (2005-2007) and bet against it via credit default swaps (CDS), generating $700M+ in profits for investors and $100M personally. This was immortalized in Michael Lewis's book and the film "The Big Short."

- **Born:** 1971
- **Education:** Vanderbilt University (medicine, neurology residency)
- **Fund:** Scion Capital (2000-2008), Scion Asset Management (2013-present)
- **Style:** Deep Value / Contrarian
- **Net Worth:** ~$300M+
- **AUM:** $154.9M (per Form ADV March 2025), expanded to ~$1.38B (Q3 2025 with options positions)

## 2. Core Philosophy

Burry's own words: *"My strategy isn't very complex. I try to buy shares of unpopular companies when they look like road kill, and sell them when they've been rehabilitated."*

### Key Tenets:
1. **"Ick" Investing:** Buy stocks that provoke a disgust reaction — hated, beaten-down, forgotten companies
2. **Margin of Safety:** Only buy when price is 30-40% below calculated intrinsic value
3. **Minimize Downside First:** On a percentage basis, replacing lost dollars is harder than gaining new ones
4. **Volatility != Risk:** Volatility creates opportunity; risk is determined by business quality, not price movement
5. **Contrarian Flexibility:** Willing to completely restructure portfolio quarter-to-quarter when data changes
6. **Deep Fundamental Analysis:** Hundreds of hours reading 10-Ks and 10-Qs

## 4. Technical Entry Rules

From his MSN Money articles:

1. **52-Week Low Proximity:** Buy within 10-15% of the 52-week low that has shown price support
2. **Bare-Bones Technical Analysis:** Mix fundamentals with simple chart analysis (held over from commodities trading days)
3. **Stop-Loss Rule:** If a stock breaks to a **new 52-week low**, **cut the loss immediately** — no exceptions
4. **Quick Profit-Taking:** Not afraid to sell when a stock has a quick 40-50% pop
5. **Portfolio Size:** 12-18 stocks diversified across depressed industries
6. **Turnover:** >50% annually (often 100-200%); tax implications are secondary

### Burry's Exact Words:
*"I prefer to buy within 10% to 15% of a 52-week low that has shown itself to offer some price support. That's the contrarian part of me. And if a stock — other than the rare birds discussed above — breaks to a new low, in most cases I cut the loss. That's the practical part."*

*"I like to hold 12 to 18 stocks diversified among various depressed industries, and tend to be fully invested."*

*"I am not afraid to sell when a stock has a quick 40% to 50% a pop."*
